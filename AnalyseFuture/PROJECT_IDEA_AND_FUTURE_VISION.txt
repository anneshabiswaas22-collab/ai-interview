AI INTERVIEW — DETAILED PRODUCT IDEA AND LONG-TERM VISION
Created: 17 November 2026

DOCUMENT STATUS

This is a product and engineering vision, not a claim that every feature already works.
The current repository contains a development candidate, experimental policy code,
documentation, and preserved third-party research projects. A feature is implemented only
after it runs in the authoritative application and passes the relevant tests.


1. THE PRODUCT IDEA

AI Interview should become a candidate-first, evidence-based interview practice platform for
software professionals. It should simulate the depth of a thoughtful human interviewer, ask
relevant follow-up questions, support text, speech, code, and system-design exercises, and turn
each session into a precise personal improvement plan.

The core promise is simple:

A candidate completes a realistic practice interview and can clearly understand:

- what they did well;
- what was incomplete or technically incorrect;
- the exact answer, code result, or design decision supporting each observation;
- what to study or practise next;
- whether their performance is improving over time.

The first market is interview preparation. Employer assessment may be explored later, but only
with separate governance, validated job-related rubrics, human oversight, accommodations, and
an appeal process. The product must never become an opaque automatic hire/reject engine.


2. THE PROBLEM

Many AI interview products can generate questions and polished-looking scores. That does not
prove that the interview was realistic or the score was correct. Common weaknesses include:

- generic questions unrelated to the candidate's prior answer;
- invented or unsupported feedback;
- shallow technical evaluation;
- poor handling of silence, interruption, reconnection, or long interviews;
- coding answers scored by a language model instead of actual tests;
- hidden privacy costs when resumes, audio, or transcripts are sent to providers;
- scores that imply false precision or penalize accent, disability, or communication style;
- vendor lock-in and unpredictable model costs.

AI Interview should compete through trust, technical depth, evidence, privacy, and measurable
usefulness rather than through the largest feature list.


3. PRIMARY USERS

Initial users:

- junior engineers preparing for their first technical interviews;
- mid-level engineers practising deeper technical discussion;
- senior and staff engineers preparing for architecture and leadership interviews;
- frontend, backend, full-stack, mobile, DevOps, data, AI/ML, and security candidates;
- candidates practising behavioural, resume-led, coding, SQL, and system-design interviews;
- candidates who need accessible text alternatives, captions, slower pacing, or repeat practice;
- non-native English speakers who want communication coaching without accent discrimination.

Later users:

- mentors and interview coaches, with candidate-controlled sharing;
- universities, bootcamps, and career programmes;
- employers and recruiters, only after tenant isolation, rubric validation, fairness review,
  human decision-making, accommodations, and legal review are complete.


4. THE FLAGSHIP EXPERIENCE

A candidate:

1. Creates an account and selects role, level, interview type, language, duration, and goals.
2. Optionally uploads a resume or job description after seeing clear privacy and retention terms.
3. Reviews what the system extracted and can remove or correct it.
4. Receives a bounded interview plan with competencies and approximate topic coverage.
5. Completes a text or voice interview with contextual, limited follow-up questions.
6. For coding, writes and runs code in an isolated executor with deterministic tests.
7. For system design, creates a diagram and explains trade-offs verbally or in text.
8. Can reconnect without losing authoritative session progress.
9. Receives a report whose important statements cite transcript events, test results, or diagram
   decisions and explicitly mark uncertainty or insufficient evidence.
10. Receives targeted drills and a learning plan, then repeats comparable practice later.
11. Controls deletion, export, sharing, transcript retention, and optional audio retention.


5. CORE DIFFERENTIATORS

A. Evidence-backed coaching

Every meaningful evaluation statement should point to exact evidence. Unsupported claims should
be rejected instead of displayed. Evidence presence alone does not prove a score is correct, so
quality still requires expert-reviewed evaluation.

B. Real technical verification

Runnable code should be judged by tests and sandbox outcomes. Architectural feedback should
refer to stated requirements and trade-offs. A language model may explain results, but should
not replace deterministic evidence.

C. Adaptive but controlled interviewing

Questions should respond to the previous answer while remaining bounded by role, rubric, time,
follow-up budget, and server-owned session state. Candidate content must never control system
policy, tools, authorization, or scoring.

D. One continuous learning journey

Interview setup, practice, transcript, feedback, drills, history, and progress should feel like
one coherent product rather than unrelated AI tools.

E. Provider flexibility

Hosted and open-weight models, speech-to-text, and text-to-speech should sit behind versioned
adapters. The product should measure quality, latency, reliability, and cost before changing
providers.

F. Privacy and accessibility

Text-only practice must remain a first-class alternative. Audio recording must be optional and
off by default. The system must not score appearance, emotion, eye contact, accent, disability,
personality, ethnicity, or vague "culture fit".

G. Honest product status

A simulated demo, local stub, or mocked provider must always be labelled. No "AI accuracy",
"production ready", "self-hosted", or "fine-tuned model" claim should be made without evidence.


6. WHAT EXISTS AND WHAT IT MEANS

The current Euron-based application is the candidate foundation. It includes useful development
components such as web UI, API, authentication, PostgreSQL, Redis jobs, provider adapters,
reports, and administration. Its default provider is simulated. It is not production-ready.

The standalone interview-core package is a useful policy experiment. It should be reconciled
with the selected application rather than becoming a second competing interview engine.

DeepInterview, GrillKit, Local AI Interviewer, and the upstream Euron archive are preserved in
AnalyseFuture as research material. They must not be merged as complete applications. Useful
concepts or small compatible components may be adopted only after license, security, quality,
maintenance, and integration review.


7. LANGUAGE AND ARCHITECTURE STRATEGY

Do not rewrite everything into one programming language merely for visual consistency.

Recommended approach:

- TypeScript as the primary product language for the web application, API, shared contracts,
  interview state, reports, and most business logic;
- Python only for isolated AI/ML, speech, model evaluation, dataset, or training workloads where
  the Python ecosystem provides a clear advantage;
- PostgreSQL as the authoritative relational database;
- Redis and the existing queue for bounded background work;
- versioned OpenAPI/JSON Schema contracts between modules;
- REST or event/job boundaries first; add gRPC only if measured requirements justify it;
- one authentication system, one session authority, one queue, and one primary database;
- model, voice, and execution providers hidden behind replaceable interfaces;
- code execution in a separate, network-restricted sandbox, never in the API process.

A possible long-term monorepo shape:

- apps/web
- apps/api
- services/interviewer
- services/evaluation
- services/voice
- services/code-runner
- packages/contracts
- packages/ui
- ml/datasets
- ml/training
- ml/evaluation
- infrastructure
- tests
- docs
- AnalyseFuture

The working foundation should move toward this shape incrementally. A clean-looking folder tree
is not worth breaking tested code or losing history.


8. FEATURES FEASIBLE WITH CURRENT TECHNOLOGY

These are realistic now, although they still require implementation and validation:

- authenticated text interview practice;
- role, level, duration, and topic setup;
- safe resume/job-description summarisation with user review;
- server-owned interview events and reconnectable sessions;
- bounded adaptive follow-ups;
- transcript-backed feedback;
- dashboard, history, export, and deletion;
- model/provider routing with budgets and timeouts;
- deterministic coding tests in an isolated executor;
- persistent system-design whiteboard;
- captions and text alternatives;
- real voice using separate speech, model, and voice adapters;
- cost, latency, error, and quality telemetry;
- original golden interview scenarios and human-reviewed model comparison;
- multilingual text practice for languages that pass quality review;
- private/self-hosted model options when hardware and quality justify them.


9. FEATURES REQUIRING RESEARCH OR INDEPENDENT VALIDATION

These are possible in principle but must not be promised before evidence exists:

- consistently human-quality follow-ups across every role and seniority;
- accurate scoring of complex architecture and leadership judgement;
- low-latency local voice on inexpensive hardware;
- reliable multilingual technical scoring across dialects and domains;
- production-grade open-weight model quality equal to leading hosted models;
- useful fine-tuning improvements over strong prompting and retrieval;
- longitudinal scores comparable across model and rubric versions;
- employer-grade assessment validity and fairness;
- robust detection of AI-assisted or plagiarised candidate responses;
- fully private on-device interview processing;
- safe automatic generation of large original question libraries;
- meaningful personalized learning recommendations over many months.

Each requires a defined benchmark, held-out data, human review, sample sizes, costs, regression
criteria, and rollback.


10. FEATURES THAT ARE NOT CURRENTLY SAFE OR TRUTHFUL TO PROMISE

The project should explicitly reject:

- guaranteed job offers or guaranteed interview success;
- perfect, bias-free, hallucination-free AI scoring;
- fully autonomous hire/reject decisions;
- mind-reading, personality detection, emotion scoring, or competence inference from faces;
- reliable deception detection from voice, gaze, or body language;
- accent scoring as a proxy for technical ability;
- secret assistance during somebody else's live interview;
- execution of untrusted candidate code inside normal application containers;
- training on private resumes, audio, or transcripts without separate informed consent;
- copying premium question banks or confidential interview material;
- unlimited free realtime voice or GPU inference with no operating cost;
- instantly converting several independent applications into one safe production system;
- training a competitive foundation model from scratch without enormous data, compute,
  expertise, evaluation, and legal resources.

Some may remain impossible to validate responsibly even if future technology makes them appear
technically possible.


11. DELIVERY ROADMAP

PHASE 0 — RECOVERY AND SOURCE OF TRUTH (0–2 MONTHS)

- Preserve all discovered material.
- Confirm Git history, licenses, notices, dependency locks, and repository integrity.
- Identify secrets or personal data without publishing their values; rotate compromised keys.
- Record every archive as research, not implemented product functionality.
- Establish one architecture decision log, feature-status matrix, threat model, and build flow.
- Keep the existing tested foundation operational while restructuring incrementally.

Exit evidence:

- reproducible clean setup and test commands;
- protected main branch and reviewed access controls;
- source/license inventory;
- no committed credentials or personal candidate data;
- documented authoritative runtime and folder ownership.

PHASE 1 — TRUSTWORTHY TEXT MVP (2–5 MONTHS)

- Stabilize authentication, ownership checks, database migrations, and queue behaviour.
- Complete role/job-description/resume setup.
- Make interview questions, events, retries, timing, and completion server-authoritative.
- Integrate evidence-backed evaluation.
- Add one real provider behind a replaceable adapter with hard budgets.
- Deliver the authenticated path from setup through saved report and history.
- Run cross-user, prompt-injection, reconnect, export, and deletion tests.

Exit evidence:

- a real user completes the full text flow;
- important report claims cite valid transcript evidence;
- provider kind, model, rubric, prompt, cost, and latency are recorded;
- failures do not silently create fake success or duplicate answers.

PHASE 2 — QUALITY LABORATORY AND COACHING (4–8 MONTHS)

- Build original golden conversations for multiple roles and answer qualities.
- Add expert review, blinded model comparisons, and regression thresholds.
- Track prompt, model, rubric, latency, token, and cost versions.
- Deliver targeted drills, improved-answer examples, and repeat-session comparison.
- Add a clear "insufficient evidence" result rather than forcing every criterion to a score.

Exit evidence:

- documented sample sizes and reviewer agreement;
- technically correct feedback meets a predeclared threshold;
- regressions block a provider/prompt promotion;
- candidate usefulness is measured in moderated studies.

PHASE 3 — REAL VOICE (6–11 MONTHS)

- Add replaceable speech-to-text, voice, and realtime providers.
- Implement captions, barge-in, silence, retry, reconnect, and transparent text fallback.
- Measure p50/p95 latency, transcript accuracy, provider failure, and cost per session.
- Test real devices, slow networks, non-native speakers, and accessibility alternatives.
- Keep audio recording separately consented and optional.

Exit evidence:

- real microphone/device sessions, not browser or provider stubs;
- measured interruption and reconnect behavior;
- accessible text/caption fallback;
- privacy and cost controls verified.

PHASE 4 — CODING AND SYSTEM DESIGN (9–15 MONTHS)

- Add an isolated code executor with language images, quotas, no normal network access, output
  limits, hostile-input testing, and deterministic grading.
- Add a persistent whiteboard with versioned diagrams and synchronized explanation.
- Create replay across transcript, code snapshots, test results, and diagram events.
- Evaluate technical feedback with experienced human reviewers.

Exit evidence:

- sandbox escape and denial-of-service threat tests;
- deterministic public/hidden test behavior;
- save/reconnect/replay of code and diagram state;
- expert-reviewed technical feedback.

PHASE 5 — CONTROLLED CANDIDATE BETA (12–18 MONTHS)

- Complete privacy, security, dependency, accessibility, and incident reviews.
- Add production observability, backups, restore tests, quotas, provider fallback, and cost alerts.
- Invite a small, consented beta group.
- Measure completion, correctness, usefulness, retention, reliability, and unit economics.
- Add simple paid practice plans only after users demonstrate real value.

Exit evidence:

- independent security findings addressed or explicitly accepted;
- restoration, deletion, export, and incident exercises;
- production service objectives and support process;
- measured candidate usefulness and sustainable cost.

PHASE 6 — MODEL OWNERSHIP AND BROADENING (15–24 MONTHS)

- Compare hosted models and licensed open-weight checkpoints on the same held-out suite.
- Create permissioned, provenance-tracked training examples.
- Attempt QLoRA only after a baseline shows a specific improvement opportunity.
- Promote a fine-tuned adapter only if blind evaluation shows meaningful benefit without
  unacceptable latency, safety, or cost regressions.
- Expand role and language packs one at a time with expert validation.
- Explore universities, coaches, and candidate-controlled sharing.

Exit evidence:

- reproducible model/data manifests;
- held-out gains against the actual baseline;
- privacy, license, safety, and cost approval;
- rollback to the previous model.

PHASE 7 — EMPLOYER TRACK (18–30+ MONTHS, SEPARATE PROGRAMME)

- Add organizations, recruiter roles, invitations, reviewer workflows, tenant isolation, and
  audit logs.
- Validate each assessment for its intended job use.
- Provide accommodations, human review, explanation, and appeals.
- Obtain jurisdiction-specific privacy, employment, and AI governance advice.
- Never reuse unvalidated candidate-practice scores as hiring decisions.

Exit evidence:

- independently reviewed tenant isolation;
- job-related validity and fairness studies;
- accountable human decision workflow;
- legal and accessibility approval for the intended markets.


12. ONE-TO-TWO-YEAR AI-ERA IDEAS

Ideas worth trying after the trustworthy foundation exists:

- a personal interview learning coach that remembers consented competency trends;
- scenario generation grounded in approved role rubrics and current technical references;
- multi-agent panel practice where separate interviewer roles cover coding, architecture,
  product judgement, and behavioural reflection;
- private local model routing for sensitive resume content;
- portable candidate-controlled practice portfolios containing evidence rather than one opaque
  number;
- mentor mode that lets a candidate share selected sessions for human annotations;
- adaptive accessibility settings learned from explicit preferences, not inferred disability;
- interview "digital twins" of target role requirements, built from authorized job data;
- incident-response, debugging, and pair-programming interview simulations;
- question packs maintained like tested software, with stable IDs, provenance, review, and
  retirement rules;
- provider routing that selects the least expensive model meeting a measured quality threshold.

These ideas are feasible to explore, but they are not promised delivery dates.


13. LONGER-TERM RESEARCH

Possible research beyond two years:

- on-device speech and interviewing with strong privacy;
- federated or privacy-preserving learning from separately consented data;
- immersive spatial/VR system-design simulations;
- collaborative incident-response and engineering-team simulations;
- verified skill portfolios controlled by candidates;
- live multilingual conversation with stable technical meaning across languages;
- simulation of complex engineering teams and ambiguous production incidents;
- open, independently audited interview-quality standards.

These are research directions, not release promises. Each must pass privacy, safety,
accessibility, cost, and human-value gates.


14. SECURITY, PRIVACY, AND FAIRNESS REQUIREMENTS

- Treat resumes, job descriptions, answers, code, diagrams, retrieved content, and model output
  as untrusted.
- Rotate any credentials suspected of exposure; never place secret values in Git.
- Require object-level authorization for every transcript, report, interview, file, and share.
- Use short-lived provider tokens and keep provider keys off the browser.
- Separate consent for necessary processing, transcript retention, audio, analytics, sharing,
  and training-data reuse.
- Redact personal data from logs and model traces.
- Scan uploads and safely parse supported formats.
- Apply rate, duration, token, audio, storage, and cost limits.
- Maintain an incident-response and backup/restore process.
- Test prompt injection, cross-user access, event replay, SSRF, parser attacks, sandbox escape,
  dependency compromise, and model-data leakage.
- Give candidates deletion, export, correction, and accessible alternatives.
- Never infer protected or sensitive traits.


15. BUSINESS MODEL

Candidate-first options:

- limited free text practice;
- paid voice minutes or longer sessions;
- role-specific practice packs;
- advanced progress and coaching plans;
- university, bootcamp, or career-service licences;
- optional mentor/coach collaboration.

Later employer options:

- organization seats;
- candidate invitations and reviewer workflows;
- validated assessment templates;
- usage-based API access.

Billing should follow demonstrated usefulness. It should not be built before provider costs,
payment eligibility, refund handling, entitlements, and regional obligations are understood.


16. SUCCESS MEASURES

Product:

- percentage completing the first interview;
- percentage reopening and understanding feedback;
- candidate-rated usefulness;
- return rate for a second practice session;
- measurable improvement on comparable rubric items.

AI quality:

- expert-agreed follow-up relevance;
- technically correct feedback assertions;
- percentage of claims with valid evidence;
- unsupported-claim and hallucination rate;
- prompt-injection success rate;
- human-review agreement and disagreement.

Reliability:

- session completion without unexpected loss;
- reconnect recovery rate;
- queue and provider failure recovery;
- p50/p95 response and voice latency;
- backup restoration success.

Safety and accessibility:

- cross-user authorization failures;
- personal-data leakage incidents;
- deletion/export completion;
- keyboard and screen-reader task completion;
- caption availability and text fallback;
- fairness and accommodation findings.

Economics:

- total cost per completed text and voice interview;
- cost by provider and modality;
- support burden;
- paid conversion and retention;
- gross margin only after infrastructure and model costs.

Initial targets are hypotheses. They must never be presented as achieved metrics without
measured sample sizes and evidence.


17. SOURCE-ADOPTION PRIORITIES

From the preserved research projects, investigate these concepts first:

1. Local AI Interviewer's deterministic evidence validation, compare-and-set transitions,
   provenance, and provider budgets.
2. DeepInterview's prep/live/post boundaries, transcript checkpoints, provider contracts, and
   rubric verifier.
3. GrillKit's question taxonomy, known-question tracking, combined theory/coding rounds, and
   public/hidden test workflow.
4. The Euron upstream snapshot only for provenance and archive-only documentation, because the
   current foundation is already its safer adapted derivative.

For every adoption:

- select one bounded behavior;
- verify the source revision and license;
- write acceptance and security tests;
- adapt it behind existing auth/database/session contracts;
- preserve attribution;
- measure quality and cost;
- update the implementation status;
- provide a rollback path.


18. DEFINING SUCCESS

The project succeeds when a real candidate can finish an actual interview, trust why the
feedback was produced, identify the next improvement, return to practise it, and retain control
of their data.

The project does not succeed merely because it has many source files, attractive screens, many
models, or a long feature list. Its defensible advantage should be trustworthy technical
coaching, measurable interview quality, privacy, accessibility, provider flexibility, and
honest evidence.
